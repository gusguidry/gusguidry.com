Here's my whole personality in one sentence: if it started as a book, I'm reading the book. If it started as a manga, I'm reading the manga. I will not be doing the anime "without the fillers." I will not be doing the Netflix adaptation with the guy from that other show. I am going to sit down with the raw material the creator actually made, and you are not going to talk me out of it.

I know this is not a rare opinion. Everyone _says_ they'd rather read the book. Almost nobody actually does it. That's the part that gets me — it's not that the take is original, it's that it's one of the easiest ways to not be lazy, and people still skip it.

Let's talk about the manga thing specifically, because this is where I get religious. You will show me a YouTube thumbnail with the title "LOOK HOW GRUESOME THE MANGA IS COMPARED TO THE ANIME" or "WOW, THEY SAID _THIS_ IN THE BOOK?!" and my reaction is always the same: yeah, no shit. Of course the manga hits different. It's the actual thing. The anime is what happens _after_ a production committee, a broadcast standards department, and whoever's writing the FCC compliance memo all take a pass at it. What you're watching is a photocopy of a photocopy, and then someone made a thumbnail acting shocked that the original ink looks better than the toner.

And don't even start with me about "just watch One Piece without the fillers." I've heard this argument my whole life. It is still, somehow, more time-consuming and less faithful than just reading the manga. You are not saving time. You are choosing the long way and calling it the short way because it has a soundtrack.

Now — I want to be clear I'm not a snob about this. I like movies. I like TV. There are adaptations I genuinely enjoy on their own terms. But as I've gotten older I've just found myself reaching for the book first, every time, and I've stopped feeling weird about it. Because here's what I actually think is going on:

**Watching an adaptation before the source is like taking a three-star dinner, dumping it in a Vitamix, and eating it as baby food.** Somebody spent years getting the seasoning right. You're drinking it through a straw because chewing felt like effort.

Media consumption in general is already the laziest thing a person can do with their evening — I'm not pretending reading a manga makes you a Rhodes Scholar. But there's a real difference between _sitting there_ while a screen tells you everything, and reading something where your brain has to build the room, the faces, the pacing, the silence between the lines. One of those is your imagination doing work. The other is a very expensive babysitter.

Now, the pushback I get every time is "the source material is hard to find." I don't buy it. If you're the kind of person reading an opinion piece on the internet, you have more than enough brainpower and willpower to go find a book. The publisher's own site probably sells it. There are used copies on eBay for a few bucks. There are manga subscription services now — plural. You can sideload an EPUB or a CBZ onto an e-reader in about four minutes. And look, I'm not condoning piracy, I'm just saying it exists and it's not exactly a locked vault either. Point is: with the amount of legitimate, dirt-cheap, low-effort options available to you in 2026, "I couldn't find it" isn't a real obstacle. It's an excuse dressed up as an obstacle.

And here's the actual payoff, beyond just "reading is good for you": once you put in that small bit of effort, you get to _actually_ say you've read The Lord of the Rings. Not "I watched the trilogy," — you _read the books_. You get to _actually_ say you've read the Dune saga. You get to _actually_ say you've read Akira, and now you can talk about what the film kept, what it cut, and why both versions are worth having an opinion about, instead of just having watched one of them and nodding along. That's not nothing. You've quietly moved yourself into a smaller, better-informed room. Whatever that's worth — and I'd argue it's worth something — you earned it for the price of a used paperback and a little bit of your evening.

So that's the opinion. It's not complicated, it's not new, and I'm sure someone's going to reply to this with "well _actually_ the anime added scenes that improved on the source" — sure, sometimes, and I'll die on the hill that those are the exception that proves the rule. Nine times out of ten, you're better off skipping the middleman and going straight to whoever actually had the idea.

Read the book. Read the manga. Then, if you want, watch the show — you'll notice everything they cut, and you'll know exactly why it doesn't hit as hard.
